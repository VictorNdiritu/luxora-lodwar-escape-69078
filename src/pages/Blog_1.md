# Travelling to Turkana? Here's What to Pack and Know Before You Go

Turkana is one of Kenya's most distinctive travel destinations. From the blue-green waters of Lake Turkana to its volcanic landscapes, ancient archaeological sites and rich cultural heritage, the region offers experiences that are quite different from the country's more familiar safari and beach destinations.

But travelling to Turkana requires some preparation. The distances between attractions can be considerable, temperatures can be high, and services become less accessible as you venture away from major towns. Knowing what to pack, what to expect and how to plan your movements can make your trip more comfortable and enjoyable.

Whether you're visiting for a weekend, travelling for work or planning a longer adventure through northern Kenya, this guide covers the essentials to help you prepare.

## 1. Understand Where You're Going

Before packing, establish which parts of Turkana you intend to visit. Turkana County is vast, and the experience varies depending on your destination.

**Lodwar** is the county headquarters and a practical starting point for arranging accommodation, supplies, transport and excursions.

**Eliye Springs** offers sandy shores and scenic views along the western side of Lake Turkana. It is a popular option for travellers seeking time by the lake.

**Kalokol** is a fishing town on the western shore where visitors can learn about the lake's fishing economy and arrange certain lake excursions.

**Loiyangalani**, on the southeastern side of Lake Turkana in Marsabit County, offers cultural experiences, desert scenery and access to the Loiyangalani Desert Museum.

**Sibiloi National Park** is known for its archaeological significance, fossil deposits and remote wilderness landscapes.

**Central Island National Park** features volcanic terrain, crater lakes, birdlife and important crocodile habitats.

These places are not all close to one another. A visit to Eliye Springs can be planned around a stay in Lodwar, while destinations such as Sibiloi and Loiyangalani require more extensive logistical planning.

Decide what interests you most before building your itinerary.

## 2. Clothing: Dress for the Climate

Turkana's hot, arid environment makes lightweight clothing a sensible choice. Breathable fabrics help keep you comfortable, while clothing that covers your skin provides useful protection from direct sunlight.

Pack the following:

- Lightweight cotton or moisture-wicking T-shirts.
- Loose-fitting trousers or lightweight hiking pants.
- Shorts for relaxing at your accommodation.
- Long-sleeved shirts for sun protection.
- Comfortable walking shoes or lightweight hiking boots.
- Sandals for use around your accommodation.
- A wide-brimmed hat or cap.
- Sunglasses with UV protection.
- Extra underwear and socks.

If you intend to visit cultural sites or local communities, modest, comfortable clothing is a practical choice. For hiking and excursions, prioritise shoes that can handle dusty paths, uneven ground and rocky terrain.

Although daytime temperatures can be high, evenings and early mornings may feel cooler depending on the season and your location. A light jacket or warm layer is useful, particularly if you're camping or spending several days outdoors.

You do not need to pack a different outfit for every activity. Choose versatile items that can be worn more than once and are comfortable for long journeys.

## 3. Sun Protection and Personal Care

The sun is one of the most important considerations when preparing for Turkana. You may spend hours in exposed landscapes, travelling between destinations or exploring places with limited shade.

Carry sunscreen with broad-spectrum protection of SPF 30 or higher, lip balm with SPF, a wide-brimmed hat and good sunglasses.

A lightweight scarf or buff is also useful for protecting your face from dust and wind, especially when travelling on unpaved roads.

Apply sunscreen before heading outdoors and reapply according to the product instructions. Whenever possible, schedule demanding activities for the cooler morning or late-afternoon hours.

Your toiletries should include:

- Toothbrush and toothpaste.
- Soap, shampoo and deodorant.
- Moisturiser and lip balm.
- Hand sanitiser and tissues.
- Wet wipes.
- Insect repellent.
- Personal hygiene products.
- A small towel.

Dry air and dust can leave your skin and lips uncomfortable, so moisturiser is worth including even if you do not normally use it at home.

## 4. Health and First-Aid Essentials

A basic first-aid kit is useful for almost any trip, but it becomes especially important when visiting remote destinations where pharmacies and medical services may be limited.

Consider packing adhesive bandages, antiseptic wipes, blister plasters, oral rehydration salts and any personal medication you may need.

If you take prescription medication, bring enough for the entire trip, plus a reasonable contingency supply where appropriate. Keep medicines in their original packaging when practical.

Consult a qualified healthcare professional before travelling if you need advice about vaccinations, malaria prevention or other destination-specific health precautions.

Hydration also deserves particular attention. Carry drinking water throughout the day and drink regularly, especially when walking or spending long periods outdoors. For longer excursions, ask your driver or guide how much water you should carry based on the route and duration.

Do not assume that water from Lake Turkana or an untreated local water source is safe to drink.

## 5. What to Carry in Your Day Bag

Your main luggage can remain at your accommodation while you explore. A smaller day bag should contain the items you may need throughout the day.

Include:

- Drinking water.
- Sunscreen and sunglasses.
- Your hat.
- A charged mobile phone.
- A power bank and charging cable.
- Some cash in Kenyan shillings.
- Identification and essential travel details.
- Basic first-aid supplies.
- Snacks.
- A camera or binoculars, if needed.
- A dust-resistant pouch for electronics.

For full-day excursions, pack enough food and water for the expected journey and allow for possible delays. If you're visiting a remote location, do not rely on finding shops or restaurants along the way.

Keep important documents and valuables secure. It is also sensible to share your planned route and expected return time with someone, particularly when travelling beyond the main towns.

## 6. Electronics, Connectivity and Photography

Turkana offers excellent opportunities for landscape photography. The lake, volcanic formations, expansive plains and changing light create striking scenery throughout the day.

A smartphone may be sufficient for casual photography, but a dedicated camera, spare batteries and additional memory cards can be useful for a longer photography trip.

Other useful items include a power bank, charging cables, a protective camera bag and a compact tripod for sunrise, sunset or night-sky photography.

Mobile coverage and charging facilities vary by location. Before leaving Lodwar for a remote destination, download offline maps, save accommodation details and ensure your devices are adequately charged.

If you plan to use a drone, check current aviation requirements and any applicable restrictions for protected areas or particular locations.

For travellers who enjoy birdwatching, binoculars are worth carrying. They allow you to observe birds and other wildlife from a safe distance without disturbing them.

## 7. Food, Water and Snacks

Food availability can vary considerably between Lodwar and more remote destinations.

Before setting off on a long excursion, establish whether meals will be available at your destination or whether you need to carry your own supplies.

Useful travel snacks include nuts, dried fruit, biscuits, crackers and energy bars. Fresh fruit that travels well can also be a good addition.

If you have dietary restrictions, let your accommodation provider or tour operator know in advance, especially if you plan to stay at a remote camp where meals may need to be organised ahead of time.

Carry drinking water in sufficient quantities and use sealed drinking water or water treated through a reliable method. Do not depend on finding bottled water along every route.

For multi-day excursions, your guide or operator should help you plan food and water requirements based on the distance, expected conditions and facilities available.

## 8. Money, Documents and Bookings

Before travelling, confirm your accommodation, transport and any planned activities. Save booking confirmations and important contact details somewhere accessible offline.

Carry your national ID or passport, as applicable, along with travel insurance information if you have cover.

Cash is useful because some remote establishments may have limited access to card facilities or mobile payment networks. Carry enough for anticipated expenses and unexpected needs, while keeping it secure.

If you're travelling by air, check baggage allowances before packing. Smaller aircraft may have different restrictions from scheduled commercial flights.

It is also useful to keep a digital copy of important documents in a secure location, separate from the originals.

## 9. Getting Around Turkana

You can travel to Lodwar by road or air, depending on your starting point and schedule. From there, your transport arrangements will depend on which attractions you want to visit.

For shorter journeys around Lodwar and the western lakeshore, a locally arranged transfer may be sufficient. More remote destinations can require a high-clearance 4x4 vehicle, experienced drivers and additional planning.

Before departing, confirm:

- Road conditions and expected journey times.
- Fuel availability along your route.
- Whether you need a 4x4 vehicle.
- Whether a local guide is required or advisable.
- Your return transport arrangements.
- Whether overnight stops are necessary.

Sibiloi National Park is particularly remote. Kenya Wildlife Service advises the use of high-clearance 4x4 vehicles and recommends travelling in convoy.

Avoid planning an itinerary that depends on every journey going perfectly. Road conditions, weather and other logistical factors can affect travel times, so leave room for delays.

## 10. Safety Tips for Your Trip

Good preparation makes it easier to enjoy Turkana responsibly.

**Manage the heat.** Drink water regularly, take breaks in shaded areas and avoid strenuous outdoor activities during the hottest part of the day where possible.

**Check local conditions.** Ask your accommodation provider, guide or relevant authorities about current road access, security conditions and any travel advisories.

**Be careful around the lake.** Lake Turkana is home to potentially dangerous wildlife, including Nile crocodiles. Do not enter the water unless a qualified local operator has explicitly confirmed that a particular activity and location are safe.

**Avoid travelling unprepared into remote areas.** Carry sufficient supplies, arrange suitable transport and share your itinerary with someone.

**Protect your belongings.** Keep valuables secure and avoid leaving electronics exposed to dust, heat or direct sunlight.

**Respect the environment.** Carry your rubbish out, minimise single-use plastics and follow instructions in protected areas.

These precautions are not intended to discourage exploration. They help you plan for the realities of travelling through a large, sparsely populated region.

## 11. Where to Stay Before Exploring

Lodwar is a practical base for travellers preparing to explore Turkana. It offers access to accommodation, supplies and transport arrangements before you travel farther into the county.

If you're flying into the region, it can be useful to settle in for a night before beginning a longer excursion. This gives you time to rest, confirm your plans and prepare for the journey ahead.

Luxora Hotel Lodwar, located adjacent to Lodwar Airport, is one accommodation option for visitors arriving in the town. Its location and complimentary 24/7 airport transfers can be useful when coordinating arrival and departure arrangements.

Wherever you stay, consider your planned itinerary, meal arrangements, transport requirements and the distance to your first destination. If you're planning a multi-day excursion, ask in advance whether luggage storage is available.

A little planning in Lodwar can make the rest of your trip easier to organise.

## 12. What to Pack for Camping

If you're planning to camp, your packing list will be longer than that of someone staying in a hotel or lodge.

Depending on the facilities provided, you may need a suitable tent, sleeping bag, sleeping mat, headlamp, cooking equipment and sufficient food and drinking water.

A reliable first-aid kit, waste bags, extra batteries and a suitable charging system are also useful.

Wind can be strong around the lake, so secure your tent properly and seek advice about suitable camping locations. Confirm campsite facilities, park regulations, fire restrictions and access arrangements before departing.

Do not assume that camping equipment, meals or other facilities will be available at every destination. Check what your operator provides and what you must bring yourself.

## 13. Must-Haves and Nice-to-Haves

### Must-haves

- Drinking water and a reusable bottle.
- Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat.
- Lightweight clothing and comfortable shoes.
- Identification and travel documents.
- Phone, charger and power bank.
- Personal medication and basic first aid.
- Cash and a backup payment option.
- Confirmed accommodation and transport.
- Offline maps and important contacts.
- Insect repellent and personal toiletries.

### Nice-to-haves

- Binoculars for wildlife and birdwatching.
- A dedicated camera and tripod.
- A lightweight scarf or buff.
- A portable fan.
- A travel pillow for long road journeys.
- A journal or sketchbook.
- A dust-resistant electronics pouch.
- Hiking poles for suitable terrain.
- A telescope for stargazing.

Your final list should reflect your plans. A traveller staying in Lodwar for two nights will need less equipment than someone undertaking a remote camping expedition.

## 14. A Final Packing Checklist

Before leaving, confirm that you have:

- [ ] Packed clothing suitable for hot, dry weather.
- [ ] Packed sun protection and comfortable footwear.
- [ ] Organised medication and first-aid supplies.
- [ ] Packed drinking water and snacks for excursions.
- [ ] Charged your phone and power bank.
- [ ] Saved maps, bookings and important contacts offline.
- [ ] Confirmed accommodation and airport transfers where needed.
- [ ] Checked transport arrangements and road conditions.
- [ ] Confirmed guides, park access and any required fees.
- [ ] Checked baggage allowances if flying.

## Final Thoughts

Turkana rewards travellers who prepare properly and take time to appreciate the region. You do not need to carry excessive luggage, but you should not underestimate the importance of hydration, sun protection, transport planning and reliable supplies.

Start by deciding which destinations interest you, then build your packing list around those activities. Allow time for the distances involved and remember that a trip to the western lakeshore is very different from an expedition into the more remote parts of the county.

If you're arriving through Lodwar, take advantage of the opportunity to organise your supplies and itinerary before heading out. With the right preparation, you'll be better placed to enjoy the Jade Sea, the landscapes of northern Kenya and the communities that make the region distinctive.
